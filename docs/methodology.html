Methodology &amp; FAQ

Methodology &amp; FAQ

A short, honest account of what the chart measures, what it estimates, and how every class and percentile is calculated.

Measurement guide

Schematic illustration, not a measured character or a reconstruction of the source’s measuring process. Circumferences wrap around the body, not across the image.

Height
Height runs from the floor to the head. The site uses effective height where appendages are included in the chart; Miyabi keeps her 170 cm chart note and uses 152.1 cm for comparison.
Bust
Bust is the circumference around the fullest chest level. Use the source value; a portrait cannot establish circumference.
Under-bust
Under-bust is the circumference below the chest. Source bra-band labels are shown separately and are not inferred conversions.
Waist
Waist describes circumference at the waist. The source’s geometry and clothing caveats remain important.
Hip
Hip is the circumference around the fullest hip/seat region. This diagram is only a location guide.
Thigh
Thigh is the recorded upper-leg circumference. A missing measurement stays unknown and is never treated as zero.

Height

Charted height comes from official listings and may include ears, footwear or accessories. The UI uses head height where the chart notes an appendage (Miyabi: 170 cm charted, 152.1 cm used). The charted value stays visible as a note; stature_cm remains the comparison field in the exports.

Circumferences

Bust, under-bust, waist, hip and thigh come from curves wrapped around the official 3D models. Clothing, armour and removed geometry make some readings approximate; the source notes are preserved verbatim.

Comparison basis

Switch between Raw measurements and Height-adjusted in the comparison dialog. Height-adjusted values express bust, under-bust, waist, hips and thighs as a percentage of effective height: Circumference ÷ height × 100. The denominator is stature_cm, including Miyabi's 152.1 cm head height. Values display to one decimal place; equal displayed values share the highlight. Waist highlights the smaller value, hips and thighs the larger value. Missing measurements stay unranked. Height, bra sizes and classes keep their existing values. Roster rankings, filters, percentiles and podiums keep their original rules; measured outfits remain separate.

Standard model vs outfit

The standard model is canonical, with one exception: Jane Doe's canonical entry uses the measured Nocturne of Light outfit. Other measured outfits are separate records with their own numbers and deltas; art-only outfits are never measured and never affect a figure, filter or ranking.

Bra sizing

The cup group follows the source's UK cup letter (A-B, C-D, DD-E, F-G, GG+). UK / US / EU letters are reproduced from the source for comparison, not as a fit recommendation.

Classes

Waist, hip and thigh classes are roster terciles of the raw circumference in cm. Build compares raw bust and hip percentiles. Shape uses all three circumferences: Hourglass needs bust/waist and hip/waist ≥1.45 with bust and hips within 15%; Straight is flare below 1.30 or bust–hip mismatch over 25%; everything else is Balanced. Thirteen agents are hand-pinned to Thick by curator read and labelled as such.

Percentiles

Bars show the roster percentile of the raw centimetre measurement, with height using head height. 0 is the smallest value in the set, 50 the midpoint and 100 the largest.

Corrections

Every character page has a pre-filled “Report an error” link. Corrections are recorded in the changelog, and the dataset carries a schema version and an update date.

Same height

Choose which rows to show and move them into your preferred order. A reference column adds signed differences: cm or inches for raw and same-height values, percentage points for height-adjusted circumferences. Same-height values use circumference ÷ effective height × reference height, assuming uniform scaling. They are hypothetical equivalents, not new measurements or anatomical predictions. Height itself always keeps its measured value. Rounded equal values share the highlight; missing values have no difference. Save named comparison sets on this device or export the visible rows and reference differences as CSV.

Find similar figures

The default weights remain bust 25, waist 20, hip 20, thigh 15 and height 20, with under-bust excluded. Custom weights range from 0 to 100. Each absolute difference is divided by that metric’s range across the full canonical roster, then averaged using the chosen weights. Missing pairs are skipped and the remaining weights are renormalized. Zero total shared weight produces no match. Height-adjusted matching uses circumference ÷ effective height × 100; selecting height still compares measured heights. Expand a result to see the differences and each metric’s contribution to its distance.

Tools

Distributions use the current filtered roster and omit missing values. Equal-width bins include the maximum in the final bin; identical values form one bin. Comparison selections appear as markers, not extra observations. Higher or Lower uses canonical measured records and judges one-decimal displayed values, including equal answers. Sandbox references are fictional, temporary and excluded from roster counts, classes, percentiles, rankings, game rounds and exports. Shared links include the sandbox inputs and tool settings. Official rankings and cup rules remain unchanged. Cup distributions group source labels in the selected UK/US/EU system, retaining full band sizes. These categories have no centimetre scaling or numeric median. UK categories keep the existing order; other labels are grouped in letter order, with repeated D forms together. Select a bar or band-by-cup cell to view its agents and add them to comparison. Band labels come from the selected source system and do not estimate volume.

Settings

Measurement settings select cm/inches and the source UK/US/EU bra sizes across the interactive pages. Preferences are saved locally and included in shared links. These are supplied source labels, with no inferred conversions. Rankings, filters and game answers retain the UK cup rule: letter first, then band, with single letters above doubled forms. Cup game rounds use full UK scores, including equal scores, and reveal UK references when another display system is selected. Language and theme are available in the same Settings window.

FAQ

Where do the numbers come from?
Heights come from official listings where available. The remaining circumferences come from curves measured around the official 3D models, so treat every value as an estimate rather than an in-game stat.
Why does Miyabi show 170 cm and 152.1 cm?
Her charted 170 cm includes her ears. The UI uses the ~152.1 cm head height as Height, while the charted number stays visible as a note and in the exports.
Why is the card value the standard model?
The standard model is the canonical figure, except Jane Doe, where the measured Nocturne of Light outfit is the only record used. Other measured outfits are stored separately with their own numbers and deltas, so no card or ranking mixes two outfits into one body.
What do the silhouette classes mean?
Shape uses bust, waist and hips: Hourglass requires both bust/waist and hip/waist ≥1.45 plus a bust–hip balance within 15%; Straight is a flare below 1.30 or a bust–hip mismatch over 25%; the rest is Balanced. Build compares the raw bust and hip percentiles. Waist, hip and thigh classes are roster terciles of their raw circumference.
What does hand-pinned mean?
A few agents read as visibly thicker or slimmer than a single circumference suggests. Thirteen are marked thick by curator read and labelled hand-pinned in their detail view.
How is the cup group calculated?
The cup group follows the source's UK cup letter (A-B, C-D, DD-E, F-G, GG+). The letter is not a fit recommendation; it is the source's sizing label.
Can I reuse the data?
The measurements are compiled from modelmetrics.net and the character art is © HoYoverse, shown via the ZZZ Fandom wiki as fair use. Link back to the source and respect those rights.
